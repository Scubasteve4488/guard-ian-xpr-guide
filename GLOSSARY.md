# Glossary: the words, in plain English, with where each comes from

Added 2026-10-21 (builder: "educated smart answers but also with the ability to explain things simply without missing info and inaccuracies"). Every entry is one or two sentences a person who has never coded can follow, then the source the meaning comes from. Numbers that change are not written here; the entry says which tool reads them live. When a word has a different meaning on another chain, the entry says so.

Sources used: the XPR Network developer skill (XPRNetwork/xpr-network-dev-skill `skill/*.md`, read at HEAD b7d9f1f on 2026-10-21), the XPR Agents skill (XPRNetwork/xpr-agents `skills/xpr-agents/SKILL.md`, HEAD 76303b0 the same day), docs.xprnetwork.org, help.xprnetwork.org, the SimpleDEX `llms-*.txt` files, and the official product pages listed in ECOSYSTEM.md. ## Accounts and keys
- **Account** — your name on XPR Network: 1 to 12 characters, lowercase letters a–z, digits 1–5 and dots (no 0, no 6–9). It is not an address made of random characters; it is a name you chose or were given. Names with dots (`xmd.token`, `eosio.token`) are system or premium names; user-registered names have no dots. *Source: dev skill `accounts-permissions.md` (name rules table); `resources.md`.*
- **Permission** — a named level of control on an account. Every account has at least two: `owner` (can change everything, including the keys) and `active` (used for day-to-day actions such as sending). Apps can be given smaller custom permissions. *Source: dev skill `accounts-permissions.md`; XPR Network's blog post on the permissions system is listed in ECOSYSTEM §15a (linked, not yet opened).*
- **Key** — the secret that signs for a permission. A public key is shared; the private key never is. WebAuth keeps the private key inside your phone's or computer's secure hardware. *Source: dev skill `webauth-identity.md` ("WebAuthn authentication… hardware-backed keys").*
- **Recovery words / seed phrase** — the 12-word phrase WebAuth gives you to rebuild your wallet on a new device; the official help article says it "can recover both accounts" (XPR and Metal). Anyone with it controls the accounts. No one legitimate ever asks for it. *Source: WebAuth help article "How do I restore an account…", updated 2026-10-05 (ECOSYSTEM §2b); RULES §5.*
- **Multisig (multi-signature)** — a permission that needs more than one key or account to approve, for example 2 of 3. Used to protect important accounts. *Source: dev skill `accounts-permissions.md`; XPR Network's blog post on 2-of-3 multisig is listed in ECOSYSTEM §15a (linked, not yet opened).*
- **Wallet drainer** — a permission or key that an attacker tricks you into adding to your account, giving them signing power over it. The fix is removing that permission with your `owner` key. *Source: Proton NZ scam alert on xprdrop, 2026-08-03 with Aug–Sep updates (ECOSYSTEM §7 XPR Auth Cleanup row and §11).*

## Resources, fees and staking
- **No gas fees** — XPR Network does not charge a fee per transaction. Users get free CPU, NET and RAM for normal use; block producers provide the capacity and are paid in XPR from inflation. *Source: XPR Network blog "How XPR Network achieves zero gas fees" (ECOSYSTEM §12b, re-read 2026-10-19); dev skill `resources.md`.*
- **CPU, NET, RAM** — the three resource allowances. CPU is processing time, NET is bandwidth, RAM is storage for the data your account keeps on chain (WebAuth accounts get 12,000 bytes free). Power users and dApps buy RAM or rent CPU/NET at resources.xprnetwork.org. *Source: dev skill `resources.md`; ECOSYSTEM §12b; live values with `xpr_account`.*
- **Staking** — locking XPR to earn rewards and to vote for block producers ("Short Stake XPR" in WebAuth; unstaking takes 24 hours). Long staking is a separate program with its own terms; read the current terms live. *Source: dev skill `staking-governance.md`; XPR Network staking and long-staking posts listed in ECOSYSTEM §15a.*
- **Block producer (BP)** — one of the organisations running the computers that make blocks and keep the chain running (XPR is delegated proof of stake: holders vote with staked XPR; 21 active producers plus standby). *Source: dev skill `staking-governance.md`; ECOSYSTEM §12b; live list with `xpr_producers`; who they are in ECOSYSTEM §10 "All active block producers".*
- **Validator (Metal Blockchain)** — the equivalent of a block producer on Metal Blockchain: a node that stakes METAL to help secure the chain (at least 2,000 METAL, per the paper and the live chain). Not the same thing as an XPR block producer. *Source: Metal Blockchain paper and live read (ECOSYSTEM §1a); live counts with `metal_blockchain`.*
- **Delegation** — on Metal Blockchain, adding your METAL (at least 25) behind a validator's stake to share its rewards without running a node; on XPR, letting a tool vote for producers with your stake. *Source: Metal Blockchain paper and live read (ECOSYSTEM §1a); ChainInfra's vote-delegation tool (ECOSYSTEM §2a).*

## Wallet and identity
- **WebAuth** — Metallicus's wallet for XPR Network and Metal: a phone app and the webauth.com web wallet. It signs with hardware-backed keys (Face ID, fingerprint, security key) rather than a password. *Source: dev skill `webauth-identity.md`; help.xprnetwork.org.*
- **Signing request** — what a website sends your wallet when it wants you to approve something; WebAuth shows it to you and you approve or reject. Read the receiver, the amount and the memo before approving. A real airdrop or claim never asks you to sign `updateauth` or `linkauth` (the actions that add or link a permission). *Source: dev skill `webauth-identity.md`; Proton NZ scam alert (ECOSYSTEM §7).*
- **KYC (identity verification)** — proving who you are with documents, done once in WebAuth through Metallicus's provider `metal.kyc`. On chain it appears as a list of verified claims (first name, last name, birthdate, address, front and back of ID or national ID number, selfie) on your account's profile row, not as a number. *Source: dev skill `webauth-identity.md` ("`kyc_level` is a claim list, not a level").*
- **Verified checkmark** — an opt-in display flag on a profile, set by a verifier account. It is not a KYC summary: fully KYC'd accounts routinely have it off. *Source: dev skill `webauth-identity.md` (`verified` field).*
- **Profile row (eosio.proton usersinfo)** — the public on-chain record of an account's display name, avatar and KYC claims. Anyone can read it on the explorer; it holds no login and no secret. *Source: dev skill `webauth-identity.md`.*
- **Metal Identity** — Metallicus's identity product "for users, systems and AI agents"; the Metal Identity app is at identity.metallicus.com. *Source: metallicus.com/institutions/metal-identity (ECOSYSTEM §1c); ECOSYSTEM §15a.*

## Tokens and money
- **Symbol + contract** — a token is only identified by both together: the ticker (XUSDC) and the account that issues it (`xtokens`). A token with the same ticker from a different contract is a different token. *Source: RULES §1; dev skill `token-creation.md`; check with `xpr_token_check`.*
- **X-token / wrapped token (XBTC, XETH, XUSDC…)** — a token on XPR that stands for a coin held elsewhere, issued by the `xtokens` contract; it came in through the Metal X Bridge and can go back out the same way. *Source: ECOSYSTEM §1 (Metal X Bridge row) and §8.*
- **Bridge** — the service that moves coins between XPR Network and other chains: the Metal X Bridge brings BTC, ETH, XRP, SOL and others onto XPR as X-tokens, with an identity check (Metal X FAQ). When the official status page shows a bridge or withdrawals paused, that is why a transfer is waiting. *Source: ECOSYSTEM §1 and §2b; `metallicus_status`.*
- **Memo** — the note field on a transfer. Contracts and exchanges read it to route your deposit: XRP, XLM, HBAR and Stellar USDC deposits need a Memo ID or the funds can be lost (help article, updated 2026-06-22); Metal X `dex` deposits need an EMPTY memo; SimpleDEX swaps are instructions written in the memo. *Source: ECOSYSTEM §2b and §12b; dev skill `simpledex.md`.*
- **Metal Dollar (XMD)** — Metallicus's dollar stablecoin, minted and redeemed 1:1 against a basket of stablecoins (USDC, PYUSD, USDP); on XPR it is `XMD@xmd.token`, 6 decimals; also on Ethereum since 2026-09-26. Buying XMD on a market is a separate trade at the market price. *Source: metaldollar.com and the official announcement (ECOSYSTEM §1, §1c, §12b); supply live with `xpr_token_check`.*
- **LOAN** — the governance and rewards token of the LOAN Protocol lending market; contract `loan.token`. *Source: dev skill `loan-protocol.md`.*
- **METAL** — the coin of Metal Blockchain (Metallicus's layer-0 chain), staked by validators; hard-capped at 666,666,666 per the paper. **MTL** is a different token: the Metal DAO token (an Ethereum token, moving to Metal L2 as gas per the "Homecoming" plan); part of METAL's supply was set aside for MTL conversion. *Source: Metal Blockchain and Metal DAO papers, Metal L2 row (ECOSYSTEM §1, §1a); live supply with `metal_blockchain`.*
- **Supply** — how many units of a token exist. Read it live; printed numbers age fast. *Source: `xpr_token_check` (get_currency_stats).*

## Trading, swaps and launches
- **DEX (decentralised exchange)** — a trading venue that runs as a contract on the chain. Metal X is an order-book DEX (limit, market, stop-loss and take-profit orders); Metal X Swap (contract `proton.swaps`) and SimpleDEX are AMMs. *Source: dev skill `metalx-dex.md`; `simpledex.md`; ECOSYSTEM §2.*
- **AMM (automated market maker) / pool** — a pot of two tokens whose ratio sets the price ("constant product"); you trade against the pot, not a person. People who add both tokens to the pot are liquidity providers (LPs) and earn a share of fees. *Source: dev skill `simpledex.md`; SimpleDEX `llms-trading.txt`.*
- **Slippage** — the difference between the price you expected and the price you got, because your trade itself moved the pool. A slippage limit makes the trade fail instead of filling at a worse price. *Source: SimpleDEX `llms-trading.txt` ("minAmountOut is compared with youReceive").*
- **Launch / bonding curve** — on SimpleDEX a new token is first sold on a curve: the price rises as more is bought. SimpleDEX is built by Proton NZ, a block producer, not by Metallicus. *Source: dev skill `simpledex.md`; SimpleDEX `llms-launches.txt`; ECOSYSTEM §2.*
- **Graduation** — the moment a launched token leaves the curve and becomes a normal trading pool on the DEX, after enough XPR has been raised and a cooling-off period has passed. The amounts and times are read live from the contract. *Source: dev skill `simpledex.md`; live with `simpledex_market` and `xpr_table` on `simplelaunch`.*
- **Sniper / snipe bot** — a program that tries to buy in the first block of a launch, before people can. SimpleDEX has anti-snipe windows (the `antisnipe` table) that limit early buys. This guide explains the mechanism and never helps build or tune a bot. *Source: dev skill `simpledex.md`; RULES §5.*

## Lending (LOAN Protocol / Metal X Lending)
- **Collateral** — the tokens you lock to be allowed to borrow. *Source: dev skill `loan-protocol.md`.*
- **Collateral factor / borrow limit** — how much you may borrow against each token, as a percentage of its value (XPR 40%, XUSDC 80%, XMD 90% when read on 2026-10-16; some tokens are 0%, so nothing can be borrowed against them; read live). *Source: ECOSYSTEM §12b; live with `loan_protocol`.*
- **Health factor** — the loan's safety score. Above 1.0 the collateral still covers the debt with margin; below 1.0 the position can be liquidated. *Source: dev skill `loan-protocol.md` (ECOSYSTEM §12b).*
- **Liquidation** — when a position's health factor falls below 1.0 the protocol lets someone else repay part of the debt and take collateral worth a little more than they paid. The borrower's debt goes down and they lose that collateral. It is the protocol's published rule, not a hack. The close factor (the share of the debt that can be repaid at once) and the liquidation bonus were both 10% in the contract's `globals` config when read on 2026-10-16; read live. The dev skill deliberately does not document how liquidations are executed, and this guide never helps build a liquidation bot. *Source: dev skill `loan-protocol.md`; ECOSYSTEM §12b; RULES §5; live with `loan_protocol`.*
- **APY** — the yearly rate, for deposits (what you earn) or borrows (what you pay), after compounding. Past rates are not a forecast. *Source: `loan_protocol` "apy" view (Metal X API).*

## Networks
- **XPR Network** — the main Metallicus chain, "a layer 1 delegated proof-of-stake (DPoS) blockchain" in its own whitepaper, built on Antelope technology (the same family as EOS, Telos and WAX): named accounts, no gas fees, fast blocks. It was called Proton before the rebrand (CoinMarketCap notes "rebranded from Proton"; Metal X Swap was Proton Swap), which is why contracts like `proton.swaps` and `eosio.proton` keep the old name; it has nothing to do with ProtonMail. *Source: XPR Network whitepaper v2.0 (ECOSYSTEM §1a); ECOSYSTEM §2, §14 and §15d.*
- **Metal Blockchain** — Metallicus's layer-0 chain, "built on Avalanche's Snow consensus family" per its own paper, with an A-Chain (Antelope/XPR), C-Chain (EVM), P-Chain (validators and subnets) and X-Chain (assets); its own validators and the METAL coin; it hosts the VMs below. *Source: the Metal Blockchain paper as summarised in ECOSYSTEM §1a; metalblockchain.org; live with `metal_blockchain`.*
- **Metal L2** — Metallicus's Ethereum layer-2, built on the OP Stack (the same software as Optimism). *Source: metall2.com and docs (ECOSYSTEM §1); live with `evm_chains`.*
- **PulseVM** — Metallicus's engine for running XPR-style (Antelope) contracts on Metal Blockchain: the code behind the "A-Chain" that is on the official roadmap. The code is public; the network is not live and no cutover date has been announced. *Source: ECOSYSTEM §1 PulseVM row, §1b roadmap, §12; community docs at pulsevm.dev.*
- **DogecoinVM / BTCVM / LTCVM** — Dogecoin, Bitcoin and Litecoin chains running on Metal Blockchain, each pegged 1:1 to the real coin through a bridge. All three are **alpha** per Metallicus's Q3 2026 report; DogecoinVM's own README (2026-10-13) says the code is unaudited, the chain has one validator and one operator holds every peg-signer key: "Keep amounts small." *Source: ECOSYSTEM §1 (BTCVM/LTCVM/DogecoinVM row) and §1a; live with `dogecoinvm_status`, `metal_vm_info`.*
- **Mainnet / testnet** — mainnet is the real chain with real money; testnet is the practice chain with free tokens from a faucet (`proton faucet:claim`). The two have different chain IDs, and contracts differ between them. *Source: dev skill `testing-debugging.md` and `resources.md`; live with `xpr_testnet`.*
- **RPC endpoint** — the web address an app or wallet uses to talk to the chain. Block producers run public ones. *Source: dev skill `rpc-queries.md`; list in ECOSYSTEM §15f.*
- **Explorer** — the website where anyone can look up any account, transaction or block: explorer.xprnetwork.org. *Source: ECOSYSTEM §1.*

## AI agents on XPR
- **XPR Agents** — the on-chain registry and job board for AI agents: agents register an account, humans post jobs or buy fixed-price services, and payment sits in escrow until the work is accepted. *Source: xpragents.com/llms.txt; xpr-agents skill; live with `xpr_agents_directory`.*
- **Escrow** — money held by the `agentescrow` contract until the job is accepted, refunded or arbitrated. Buying a listing means one transfer to `agentescrow` with the memo `buy:<listing id>`, never a direct payment to the agent. *Source: xpr-agents skill "How a purchase arrives".*
- **Trust score** — XPR Agents' 0–100 score for an agent, made of four parts: the owner's KYC level (up to 30), XPR staked (up to 20), KYC-weighted feedback from other agents (up to 40) and months active (up to 10). *Source: XPR Agents skill, "Trust Score System" @ 76303b0.*
- **Owner KYC level** — the identity-verification level of the agent's human owner, as the agent registry records it; the agent inherits it for its trust score ("a new agent with a KYC Level 3 owner starts with 30 points"). The agent account itself usually has no KYC row of its own. *Source: XPR Agents skill ("Agents inherit KYC from their human owner"); `xpr_account_check`.*
- **A2A** — agent-to-agent: the protocol by which one agent asks another for work. *Source: XPRNetwork/xpr-agents `docs/A2A.md` (listed in ECOSYSTEM §15a).*

## Safety words this guide uses
- **Verified** — read from the chain, an official page or a feed, and quoted with the source's own date. **Reported** — a press or community source said it; worded as that source's view ("<Outlet> reported on <date> that…"). **Unverified / "I haven't verified that"** — no source covers it; the guide names where it could be checked and never fills the gap from memory. *Source: RULES §1 and §2.*
- **Record, not verdict** — the guide shows the facts behind an account, a token or a site and names what it cannot see. It never says "safe" or "scam" in its own voice. *Source: RULES §3; `xpr_account_check`.*
- **Blocklist** — the on-chain `blocklist` contract's table of blocked accounts (actions `adduser`, `removeuser`, `pausebl`; ABI read 2026-10-21). The guide tells you whether an account you name is on it, and never lists the names itself. *Source: `xpr_account_check` (its source notes); RULES §3.*
